Caveat on figure hashes: matplotlib writes a creation timestamp into every PDF,
so `figures/*.pdf` hashes change between runs even when the plotted data are
identical. The CSV hashes above are the deterministic ones; the PNG previews
carry a timestamp-free pixel payload and are stable for a fixed matplotlib
version. Reproducibility therefore means "the CSV values and the plotted
quantities are identical", not "the PDF bytes are identical".

6. KNOWN NON-DETERMINISM AND FAILURE MODES
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
- `verify_refs.py` needs network access. It fails loudly, with a per-entry
  reason in data/ref_rejected.csv, when a DOI is unreachable or a title search
  returns nothing above threshold. It is excluded from the default `run_all.py`
  chain for that reason and enabled with `--refs`.
- Crossref occasionally rate-limits; the script retries with a delay and
  records the failure rather than silently dropping a reference.
- The three dataset entries that carry a title and a DOI but no personal
  author now carry the data centre as a corporate author ({PANGAEA},
  {ORNL DAAC}, {GPCC}), so that author-year sorting and the in-text label
  ("PANGAEA, 2021") are well defined. `bibtex` runs without warnings.
- The literature values that enter Table 1 and Table S1 are fixed constants in
  `code/phi_core.py`. Changing a source value changes every downstream table,
  both documents and Figs. 1, 2, 3, S2 and S3 in one run; there is no second
  place where a number is typed by hand.
- The PDFs are produced by `run_all.py` only if `pdflatex` is on PATH;
  otherwise the stage is skipped with a message and the rest of the chain still
  completes. Both documents need their own bibtex pass (`manuscript.bbl`,
  `supplement.bbl`).
- `verify_refs.py` regenerates refs.bib from its internal candidate list, so a
  `--refs` run restores keys that were deleted during compression. After such a
  run, trim refs.bib back to the key set that the two documents cite before
  committing; the committed file (54 keys, 54 typeset) is the post-trim state.
- Byte-identity of the committed CSVs and figures holds for the pinned
  reference versions in section 1. An anonymous clean clone on 2026-11-09
  resolved numpy 2.5.4, pandas 3.1.1, matplotlib 3.11.2 and scipy 1.18.1:
  still 9 passed, all headline numbers unchanged, but `data/power_curve.csv`
  and the seven figures then differ in bytes (the power-curve values agree to
  2.4 x 10^-16; the figures differ by matplotlib version). The numbers, not
  the bytes, are what the manuscript claims.
- The word counts in section 4 are token counts of the TeX source, not of a
  Word file. A publisher's counter may differ by a few per cent, in either
  direction, because it may charge inline formulas as words and may hyphenate
  differently. The 95-word margin is the allowance for that.
